## P-001 · Count prioritisation

DeHoratius, Holzapfel, Kuhn, Mersereau & Sternbeck (2023; online 2022), [Evaluating Count Prioritization Procedures for Improving Inventory Accuracy in Retail Stores](https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.2022.1119). *M&SOM* 25(1), 288–306. [Institutional record](https://edoc.ku.de/id/eprint/27376/) marks a peer-reviewed journal; the article acknowledges two reviewers.

**Question/design/context:** Compare rule/model count priorities using historical simulation against physical audits. Archival data covered 150 Bavarian dm stores/5,000 items; audits covered approximately 500 common items in 10 convenience-selected stores, twice weekly for eight weeks in 2017. Duplicate student counts and sales-timing adjustments supported measurement.

**Measures/findings:** Discrepancy and unknown-stockout detection at fixed counting depths favour different policies. Simple sales-based prioritisation competed with Bayesian methods for discrepancy detection; low-stock ranking performed better for unknown stockouts. These are historical policy comparisons, not a randomised app intervention.

**Limits/transfer:** One chain/time; geographic convenience selection, existing correction practices, correlated counts and objective-dependent results restrict SME transfer. Projected sales gains are modelled, not realised causal benefits. Supplements were not independently audited. Do not assume AI is necessary or claim a universal best count policy.

**Decision/source locations:** Keep count objectives explicit; defer automated prioritisation pending representative data and baseline comparison. Methods §§3–4, results §§5–6, limitations §7; PDF pp. 5–11, 15, 17–19. Figure 5 visually checked. Private source SHA-256: `51e061952b1a3b0465905bc79b587fca4e0d1b1f3f71a5c4cd74ce40032c9255`.

## P-002 · Measuring stock-record errors

Glock, Syntetos & Rekik (2025), [On the measurement of inventory record inaccuracies](https://doi.org/10.1080/09593969.2025.2608729). *International Review of Retail, Distribution and Consumer Research*. [Institutional record](https://research.em-lyon.com/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/On-the-measurement-of-inventory-record/9940215609453?institution=33EML_INST) identifies peer review and open access.

**Question/design/context:** Review measurement choices, interview 25 retail experts and discuss findings with 46 workshop participants. A Scopus/snowball search retained 37 papers. Recruitment used ECR/professional networks; some workshop participants overlapped interviews.

**Measures/findings:** Signed, absolute, binary, tolerance and percentage measures capture different properties. Signed totals can cancel errors; zero denominators and asymmetric percentages complicate interpretation. Purpose, scale, interpretability and operational action matter. This is methodological/qualitative evidence, not measured product effectiveness.

**Limits/transfer:** English/access restrictions, purposive expert selection, researcher interpretation and overlapping member validation limit representativeness and independence. Sensitive interview data are unavailable. Metric recommendations need contextual evaluation; they do not prove business benefit or legal compliance.

**Decision/source locations:** Show per-item physical-minus-expected differences with units. Do not sum kg and pieces, hide missing counts as zeros, or manufacture an overall accuracy score. Methods PDF pp. 4–7; metric discussion pp. 9–15; implications pp. 20–22; data availability p. 23. Signed-error formula/example visually checked on p. 9.
